# OLORIC v0.4 — Root-Cause Audit of v0.3 Remaining Weaknesses

**Date**: 2026-09-27  
**Subject**: Root-cause audit of v0.3 residual failures and supplemental benchmark design for v0.4.  
**Status**: Read-only audit. v0.3 dataset, splits, checkpoint, benchmark, and evaluation artifacts are frozen and unmodified.  
**Scope**: `evaluation/benchmark.jsonl`, `evaluation/oloric-v0.3/responses.jsonl`, `evaluation/oloric-v0.3/scores_semantic.jsonl`, `data/generated/oloric_v03_dataset.jsonl`, `scripts/generate_v03_dataset.py`, `scripts/v03_concept_registry.py`.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

v0.3 resolved three of the four v0.2 root-cause failures:
- **FC-01 (cross-domain caloric bleed)**: Fully resolved — zero caloric formula contamination.
- **FC-02 (task-type following collapse)**: Substantially resolved — 18/20 task types correctly matched.
- **FC-04 (misconception correction incoherence)**: Fully resolved — valid counterarguments produced.

Two weaknesses remain:
- **FC-03 (prior-hint error propagation)**: Persists. Traced to a **context injection defect** in the benchmark's own `conversation_context`, not in v0.3 training data.
- **Document Grounding (1.15/4 mean)**: Persists. Traced to three compounding root causes: (a) **benchmark gap** (0/20 dedicated document grounding tests), (b) **concept-set mismatch** (12/20 benchmark concepts absent from v0.3 training), and (c) **training supervision gap** (24 `document_grounded` records only 5% of dataset; retrieved evidence is unused metadata in 95% of responses).

## 2. Part 1 — FC-03 Root-Cause Audit (Prior-Hint Error Propagation)

### 2.1 Affected Benchmark Examples

| ID | Category | Domain | Dimension Affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nutrition_food_science_hint_based_teaching_007` | hint_based_teaching | nutrition_food_science | factual_correctness=2, strategy_switching=2 |

**Only 1 benchmark example is affected.**

### 2.2 Exact v0.3 Generated Response

```
action: explain
strategy: hint
response: "Instead of giving you the answer directly, let me guide you.
  Hint 1: it is defined by BMI = weight(kg)/height(m)².
  Hint 2: Think about how Food Preservation relates to basic biology.
  Hint 3: Consider this real-world scenario: Canning vegetables involves
          heating them to kill bacteria, then sealing them in jars to
          create an airtight environment.
  Now try to put these pieces together to explain Food Preservation in
  your own words."
understanding_check.expected_answer: "the process of extending the shelf
  life of food by inhibiting the growth of microorganisms and slowing
  down chemical reactions that cause spoilage."
```

**Key observation**: Hint 3 and `understanding_check.expected_answer` are factually correct Food Preservation content. Only Hint 1 is wrong. The model's understanding_check no longer splices BMI into the definition (improvement over v0.2).

### 2.3 Defect Localization: Where Does "BMI = Food Preservation" Appear?

| Source | Contains BMI / Wrong Content? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **v0.3 training dataset** (480 records) | **NO** | 0 records with `strategy=hint` contain BMI. 0 hint records teach Food Preservation as a concept. |
| **v0.3 target responses for hint** | **NO** | 24 hint records have factually correct, concept-specific content (e.g., MPC, Newton's Laws, Photosynthesis) |
| **v0.3 benchmark context: `conversation_context`** | **YES — ROOT CAUSE** | Prior tutor turn in benchmark directly states `"Sure! Hint 1: it is defined by BMI = weight(kg)/height(m)²."` |
| **v0.3 benchmark context: `retrieved_evidence`** | Partial | Contains `BMR = 10×weight(kg) + 6.25×height(cm) - 5×age(yr) + s` and `Energy intake = Energy expenditure + Δbody stores` — related to body metrics but not the source of the BMI string |
| **v0.3 benchmark target response** | YES (wrong target) | The **frozen benchmark target** contains `"Hint 1: it is defined by BMI = weight(kg)/height(m)²"` — the original v0.2-era generator error embedded into the benchmark |
| **v0.3 concept registry** | NO | Food Preservation is absent from `scripts/v03_concept_registry.py` |
| **v0.3 training: hint records** | NO | 0 hint records mention BMI |

### 2.4 Root Cause Classification

**FC-03 is a CONTEXT INJECTION DEFECT in the benchmark itself, not a v0.3 training defect.**

**Mechanism**:
1. The benchmark `nutrition_food_science_hint_based_teaching_007` has a `conversation_context` where a prior tutor turn states verbatim: `"Sure! Hint 1: it is defined by BMI = weight(kg)/height(m)²."` — a factually incorrect hint that was generated by the v0.2-era generator (same defect that `fix_seed_data_v02.py` produced via the `det_get_concept_example` hash-modulo fallback).
2. The benchmark target response perpetuates this: `"Hint 1: it is defined by BMI = weight(kg)/height(m)²"` appears in the frozen `target.response`.
3. When v0.3 runs inference on this example, the conversation_context is injected into the ChatML prompt. The model sees a prior tutor turn containing the BMI formula, and generates a Hint 1 that matches the conversational context — producing the identical wrong string.
4. This is **in-context propagation from a corrupt benchmark example**, not a training artifact.

**Defect Type: EVALUATION ARTIFACT / BENCHMARK CONTEXT DEFECT**

The correct v0.3 training data for hint_based_teaching contains:
- 24 records across 24 concepts (MPC, Newton's Laws, Photosynthesis, Glycemic Index, etc.)
- **Zero records for Food Preservation** (Food Preservation is not in the v0.3 concept registry)
- **Zero records with BMI in conversation_context**
- All 24 hint target responses are factually correct

### 2.5 Quantitative Impact

| Scope | BMI-contaminated records |
|---|---|
| v0.3 training dataset (480 records) | **0** |
| v0.3 training hint records (24 records) | **0** |
| v0.3 benchmark (20 records) | **1** (benchmark_007 — context_contaminated) |
| v0.3 responses (20 responses) | **1** (perpetuated from benchmark context) |

### 2.6 Sub-Defect: Food Preservation Concept Gap

**A related but distinct issue**: Food Preservation is one of 12 benchmark concepts that are completely absent from the v0.3 training dataset.

**Benchmark concepts NOT in v0.3 training**:
```
Cell Mitosis, Effective Communication, Food Preservation, Enzyme Activity,
Monetary Policy, Dietary Fiber, Matrix Operations, Bias Identification,
Nutrient Deficiencies, Linear Algebra, Learning Strategies, Audit Procedures
```

This means the model has no training supervision for hint-based teaching of Food Preservation. When it encounters the benchmark's hint_based_teaching_007, it must generalize from its training on other hint concepts. The model's Hint 3 (canning vegetables) demonstrates correct generalization. Hint 1 perpetuates the corrupt prior context.

**v0.4 Remedy**:
1. Expand the v0.4 concept registry to include all 20 benchmark concepts (or a superset).
2. Include a Food Preservation hint_based_teaching training record with correct content.
3. Include training examples where a prior tutor hint is explicitly wrong and the model must correct it: `"I notice my previous hint contained an error. Let me correct Hint 1: ..."` — prior-error detection behavior.
4. Optionally: Repair the benchmark's corrupted `conversation_context` in the Food Preservation example (not `evaluation/benchmark.jsonl` — the frozen benchmark — but by creating a corrected benchmark addendum).

## 3. Part 2 — Document Grounding Root-Cause Audit

### 3.1 Benchmark Coverage

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total benchmark examples | 20 |
| Category `document_grounded` in benchmark | **0** |
| Examples with generic `selected_text` only ("fundamental to understanding...") | **20 / 20** |
| Examples with real retrieved_evidence formulas (MPC=ΔC/ΔY, etc.) | **19 / 20** (1 has only "Key principle...") |
| Examples where evidence is concept-relevant | **Partial** — formulas are real but often from adjacent concepts |
| Examples that _explicitly require_ grounding for full credit | **0** |

**Finding**: The benchmark has zero dedicated document grounding test cases. All 20 benchmark examples use a generic `selected_text` template ("The concept of X is fundamental to understanding Y...") that contains no citable content. The `retrieved_evidence` fields contain domain formulas, but no benchmark task requires the model to cite them — the evidence is unreferenced metadata.

### 3.2 Evidence Citation in v0.3 Responses

All 20 v0.3 responses: `cites_evidence=False`, `cites_doc_words=False`.

Not a single response explicitly references `retrieved_evidence` or quotes from `selected_text`. This is consistent with the training signal: 95% of training records (456/480) have no document grounding in their target responses.

### 3.3 V0.3 Training Supervision Quality

**Document-grounded training records (24/480, 5%)**:

Representative sample:
```
ID: v03_economics_document_grounded_001
selected_text: "According to the textbook (p.45): 'The MPC measures the
               proportion of each additional dollar of income that is
               devoted to consumption expenditure.'"
retrieved_evidence: ["MPC = ΔC/ΔY", "Multiplier = 1/(1−MPC)"]

target.response: "Let's look at what the document says about MPC.
According to the textbook (p.45): 'The MPC measures the proportion of
each additional dollar of income that is devoted to consumption
expenditure.' Furthermore, Table 3.2 shows that households with income
below $30,000 ..."
```

**Assessment**: The v0.3 `document_grounded` training records are correctly structured:
- `selected_text` contains a real quotable passage
- `retrieved_evidence` contains domain-relevant formulas
- `target.response` explicitly quotes `selected_text`
- `target.strategy = "document_grounded"` (corrected from v0.2's `simple_explanation` mislabel)

**The v0.3 training data quality for document_grounded is correct.** The supervision signal exists but is insufficient in two ways:

1. **Volume**: 24 records = 5% of the dataset. The model is supervised for grounding in 1/20 training cases.
2. **Benchmark gap**: The benchmark has 0 dedicated document_grounded test items. The model was trained on grounding but never _evaluated_ on it in a grounding-specific context.

### 3.4 Concept Set Mismatch Impact on Grounding

12/20 benchmark concepts are absent from v0.3 training. This means:
- When the model generates a response for `Bias Identification` (not in training), it has no training signal for what to ground in the document for that concept.
- Even if the model had learned "quote the retrieved evidence," it has no parametric knowledge of Bias Identification to anchor the citation correctly.

The concept gap compounds the grounding gap: **the model cannot ground evidence it doesn't recognize as relevant to the concept**.

### 3.5 Root Cause Classification

Document grounding weakness is caused by **three compounding defects**:

| Defect | Type | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **DG-1**: 0/20 benchmark examples require document grounding | Benchmark coverage gap | Cannot measure grounding performance with current benchmark |
| **DG-2**: 12/20 benchmark concepts absent from v0.3 training | Concept set mismatch | Model must generalize to unseen concepts without anchoring |
| **DG-3**: 5% of training records (24/480) are document_grounded | Training supervision underdensity | Model learned to ignore retrieved_evidence in 95% of cases |

**Primary root cause**: **DG-1 — Benchmark coverage gap** is the dominant factor. The model achieves 1.15/4 because the benchmark doesn't test grounding: every response is scored on whether it uses unreferenced metadata that no task requires it to use.

**Secondary root cause**: **DG-3 — Training supervision underdensity**. Even when the model should ground, the training signal (5% of data) is too sparse relative to non-grounding supervision (95%).

**Tertiary root cause**: **DG-2 — Concept set mismatch**. Even correctly trained grounding behavior cannot transfer to unseen concepts without concept-specific factual knowledge.

## 4. Part 3 — Supplemental Document Grounding Benchmark Design

### 4.1 Design Objectives

The supplemental benchmark must:
1. Provide explicit evaluation signal for document grounding where the original benchmark provides none.
2. Test five distinct grounding behaviors (evidence use, paraphrase, evidence-based explanation, conflict handling, refusal to invent).
3. Use realistic document contexts with genuine citable content.
4. Not modify `evaluation/benchmark.jsonl`.
5. Cover 20–30 examples with balanced domain and task-type distribution.

### 4.2 Proposed Benchmark: `evaluation/benchmark_docground_v1.jsonl`

**Target**: 25 examples (5 per grounding behavior class).

#### Class A: Direct Evidence Use (5 examples)
The student asks a factual question and the correct answer is directly contained in `selected_text` or `retrieved_evidence`. The model must quote or paraphrase with citation.

```json
{
  "id": "dg_economics_direct_001",
  "class": "direct_evidence_use",
  "document_context": {
    "document_id": "macro_econ_ch3",
    "title": "Principles of Macroeconomics",
    "page": 47,
    "section": "Fiscal Multipliers",
    "selected_text": "The fiscal multiplier measures the change in real GDP per dollar of additional government spending. Empirical estimates suggest multipliers between 0.5 and 2.0 depending on the state of the economy.",
    "retrieved_evidence": ["Multiplier = 1/(1-MPC)", "MPC = 0.8 implies multiplier = 5.0"]
  },
  "student_question": "The textbook says something about multipliers. What does it actually say?",
  "expected_grounding_behavior": "Quote selected_text verbatim or near-verbatim, then apply the formula from retrieved_evidence.",
  "scoring_criteria": "Score 0 if student question answered without reference to document. Score 3-4 if selected_text quoted and formula operationalized."
}
```

**5 examples** covering: economics (fiscal multiplier), mathematics (integration by parts), science (cell biology), nutrition (glycemic response), accountancy (depreciation).

#### Class B: Paraphrase Grounded in Evidence (5 examples)
Selected_text is available; task is to explain the concept in simpler terms while remaining grounded (paraphrase without hallucination).

Student prompt style: "Can you explain this in simpler terms?" with a rich `selected_text`.

**Scoring**: Grounded paraphrase (preserves document meaning, simplifies language) vs. ungrounded generic explanation.

#### Class C: Evidence-Based Explanation (5 examples)
`retrieved_evidence` contains a formula or data point. Student asks why a concept works. Correct answer operationalizes the formula from `retrieved_evidence`.

Student prompt style: "Why does [concept] work the way it does?" with `retrieved_evidence` containing the mechanistic formula.

**5 examples**: MPC multiplier mechanism, Newton's 2nd Law derivation, stoichiometry ratios, protein synthesis codon table, depreciation schedule.

#### Class D: Evidence Conflict Handling (5 examples)
`selected_text` and `retrieved_evidence` contain conflicting information (e.g., different values for the same quantity). Model must detect and surface the conflict rather than silently choosing one.

Student prompt: "What does the document say about X?" where `selected_text` states X=A but `retrieved_evidence` states X=B.

**Expected behavior**: Model acknowledges the discrepancy and asks for clarification or notes both values.

**5 examples**: Conflicting depreciation rates, conflicting MPC values, conflicting cell division counts, conflicting energy intake calculations, conflicting financial ratios.

#### Class E: Refusal to Invent Unsupported Claims (5 examples)
`selected_text` is limited/generic and `retrieved_evidence` does not contain information needed to answer the student question. Model must decline to invent and instead direct the student to seek additional sources.

Student prompt: specific factual question that cannot be answered from the provided context.

**Expected behavior**: Model states it cannot answer from the provided document excerpt and suggests where to find the answer.

**5 examples**: Asking for a specific statistic not in evidence, asking for a mechanism not described in selected_text, asking for a definition that is only implied, asking for historical data not provided, asking for comparative analysis requiring external knowledge.

### 4.5 Scoring Rubric Modification for Supplemental Benchmark

The existing 0–4 document_grounding scale applies, but the supplemental benchmark adds Class-specific pass criteria:

| Score | Class A | Class B | Class C | Class D | Class E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | No evidence used | Generic answer | No formula used | Silently picks one value | Invents unsupported claim |
| 1 | Evidence mentioned but not quoted | Paraphrase drifts from document | Formula mentioned, not used | Notes discrepancy vaguely | Hedges but still invents |
| 2 | Partial quote or paraphrase | Mostly grounded, minor drift | Formula operationalized partially | Detects conflict, cites both values | Correctly identifies limits |
| 3 | Accurate quote + interpretation | Accurate grounded paraphrase | Formula correctly operationalized | Conflict surfaced + asks for clarification | Declines to answer + suggests source |
| 4 | Accurate quote + operationalization + verification question | As above + verification check | As above + application example | As above + explains implications of conflict | As above + partially answers what is supported |

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior-error-detection training overfits**: Model learns to always correct Hint 1, even when correct | Medium | Balance correct-hint records (majority) with error-correction records (minority); test on benchmark after training |
| **Document grounding overcorrection**: Model starts citing evidence in tasks where evidence is irrelevant | Medium | Ensure grounding instruction is task-conditional: only required for `document_grounded` tasks, not hint/practice/follow_up |
| **Concept set expansion introduces new bleed**: Adding 20+ new concepts may reintroduce hash-modulo or cross-concept contamination | Medium | Run strict content validator after dataset generation; verify per-concept evidence fields are concept-specific |
| **Supplemental benchmark contamination**: If `benchmark_docground_v1.jsonl` examples are too similar to training records, they will overfit | High | Ensure supplemental benchmark concepts differ from training records; use held-out concepts or out-of-domain examples |
| **Corrupt benchmark context (benchmark_007) not repaired**: FC-03 will persist through v0.4 if the benchmark context is left unchanged | Medium | Propose a `benchmark_corrected.jsonl` addendum that fixes `conversation_context` for benchmark_007 OR treat benchmark_007 as an adversarial test (context-repair test) |
